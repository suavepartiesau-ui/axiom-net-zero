OUR BASELINE

About.

We believe the world already possesses the energy and resources required to sustain every community. The problem was never scarcity. It's the infrastructure standing between what exists and who needs it.

The Problem We Saw

Plumbing, not scarcity.

Every neighbourhood we looked at had enough. Enough sunlight falling on enough roofs. Enough people willing to give an hour of their time. Enough data already being collected about the air and soil around them. What was missing wasn't the resource — it was the connective tissue that moves a resource from where it's generated to where it's needed.

We call that connective tissue "the pipes and plumbing." It's the layer of intermediaries, approval processes, and disconnected systems that a resource has to pass through before it reaches a person — and every layer it passes through is a place where it can leak, stall, or simply never arrive.

Axiom Net Zero exists to shorten that distance. Not by generating more, but by wasting less of what's already there.

How we're structured.

Axiom Net Zero is a decentralised collective, not a company with a head office. We don't ask permission to fix the connections in our own neighbourhoods, and we don't need a central authority to coordinate them. Four principles hold that structure together.

Node Autonomy

Each community node — a solar cluster, a sensor network, a group of volunteers — operates independently. No node needs sign-off from another to act.

Shared Ledger

What each node produces and distributes is recorded on the same public ledger, so independence doesn't come at the cost of accountability.

Open Participation

Anyone can stand up a node or contribute hours. There's no membership fee and no gatekeeping committee deciding who's allowed to help.

No Extraction

We don't take a margin on the resources that move through us. What a node generates is what its community receives.

Our Position

"We're not asking anyone to consume less or want less. We're asking why so much of what's already generated never reaches the people it was meant for — and then we go and fix that specific leak."
